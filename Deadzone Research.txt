Deadzone Research
Author: Tot_sr
Scope: GamePad and original Wii U Pro Controller paths examined for WWHD Deadzone Tool.

The useful distinction is between stick calibration and the deadzone itself.
A center/origin is the reading the input library treats as neutral. An inner
clamp/deadzone suppresses small movement around that neutral value. An outer
clamp sets where the reported stick reaches its normalized maximum. Together
with the two-axis clamp mode and any game-side processing, these determine what
movement reaches gameplay.

A simplified pipeline is:
physical stick -> raw reading -> subtract stored center -> clamp/normalize -> game

Changing the stored center can change where a fixed deadzone lies physically,
even if its numerical threshold stays the same. For example, in a simplified
one-axis calculation a raw reading of 2320 becomes 272 with center 2048, or 240
with center 2080. With an inner threshold of 258, the first can register movement
while the second remains neutral. These are illustrative numbers, not a measured
response curve or a claim about every diagonal/clamping path.

Library initialization or an explicit recalibration request can reacquire stick
centers. A game launch can therefore change effective behavior while leaving the
clamp thresholds unchanged. Our findings do not establish that every Wii U game
randomizes or changes its deadzone size on every launch.

GamePad
-------
The GamePad path uses vpadbase for lower-level input/calibration and vpad for
processed stick values. Analysis identified four center offsets, calibration
readiness, an origin-lock control, left/right max/min thresholds and cross-clamp
mode. The tool captures the existing centers; it does not calculate a new center
from the stick position at the instant A is pressed.

The readiness byte and origin-lock word serve different purposes. An initialized
controller can have valid centers with the lock cleared. Captured profiles lock
the saved centers against automatic replacement when replaying them.

The examined clamp setters enforce a minimum of 258 and maximum of 919. A zero
inner threshold is represented through separate zero-clamp disable APIs; passing
zero to the normal threshold setter alone does not preserve that disabled state.
These values are library units, not universal percentages of physical stick travel.

Original Wii U Pro Controller
----------------------------
The examined padscore path decodes the report's four stick axes, maintains native
per-controller centers and subtracts them before processed input reaches KPAD.
The centers are stored separately from the GamePad's calibration. The plugin
matches a saved Pro snapshot to that physical controller's Bluetooth identity,
so moving it to another player channel does not assign another pad's centers.

In this library revision, the Pro normalization thresholds/mode are shared.
The tool checks them against the capture and refuses a mismatch instead of
silently overwriting shared settings. It restores only the identified native
center fields for the matching original Wii U Pro Controller.

What the plugin does
--------------------
A profile stores one GamePad and one Pro capture. The selected profile is
reapplied automatically in supported WWHD launches once the input libraries and
controller initialization are ready. Repeated readbacks detect when native state
needs restoration. No manual restore button or menu visit is required for replay.
A disconnected Pro is handled when its matching controller becomes ready.
Older GamePad captures need one recapture because they saved thresholds only.

Evidence and limits
-------------------
On one Wii U running TWWHD-GZ, saved profiles were compared before and after a
full restart without recapturing. Both controllers' native center readbacks
matched their captures, each recorded a restore write, and stick movement was
observed on both. GamePad clamps, origin lock and cross mode also matched.
The tool was also tested on a vanilla European copy of The Wind Waker HD,
as reported by Tot_sr. The detailed before/after telemetry checks above were
performed on TWWHD-GZ; equivalent telemetry was not collected for the vanilla test.

These results verify the tested cases, rather than every firmware/library revision
or controller. They do not establish a complete physical response curve or
first-buffered-sample equivalence. Games can apply additional input filtering.
